The model, in one line

You source candidates for free, get them ready, and put them in front of companies. Companies pay you a fee when someone joins. Training is the optional add-on candidates can pay for. Keep training money and placement promises separate — sell training as training, never as a guaranteed job.

How to use this — the whole thing in plain words

New here, or handing this to Sanju or Shashank? Read this once. Every tab explained, plus the daily routine. Tap any row to open it.

The one-line model: a company has a job → you add it as an Opening → you match a ready candidate to it → you move them along the pipeline → they join → the company pays you a fee. Candidates never pay you.

The real, legitimate boards recruiters in India actually use.

Spot a fake / scam posting (protect your candidates):
  • Asks the candidate to pay a fee, deposit, or "registration" — real jobs never charge the applicant.
  • Offers a salary way above market for little experience.
  • Contact is a personal Gmail/WhatsApp only, no company domain email.
  • Vague company name, no website, no real office.
  • Pushes you to act "immediately", asks for Aadhaar/bank details up front.
If it smells off, don't forward it to a candidate. Your name is on it.

HR Outreach Playbook

How to find the right HR, reach them, talk money, and not get cheated. Read once, refer back often.

1. The mindset that wins

You're not "asking HR to empanel you." You're a vendor with a product: a ready candidate who fits a real opening. The warmest opener is never "we're a new consultancy, please add us." It's:

"I have a PL/SQL developer, 3 years, available now, open to Bangalore or WFH. Do you have a need?"

Lead with the candidate, never the service. Companies don't care that you exist. They care that you can fill a seat today.

2. Pick the RIGHT person (most people message the wrong one)
  • Talent Acquisition / Recruiter / TA — owns the open jobs. This is your person.
  • Hiring Manager (e.g. "Database Lead") — needs the person directly. Often faster at small companies.
  • HR Generalist / HR Manager — payroll, policy, attendance. Does not own openings. Messaging them goes nowhere.

Pick the right kind of company too. Big MNCs won't talk to an unknown agency (months of empanelment). Start where the door is open:

  • BPOs hiring in bulk — TA teams always short, easiest entry.
  • IT services / staffing firms — be a sub-vendor, take their overflow roles, place immediately.
  • Startups / SMEs — no internal recruiting team, they need you more.
3. How to find them

A company posting a job right now is the strongest buying signal there is.

  • Your own network first. One warm intro beats 100 cold messages. List everyone you know in HR or who runs a company.
  • Naukri / LinkedIn job posts — find an open role, then find who posted it.
  • LinkedIn search: "Talent Acquisition" or "Recruiter" + company or city.
4. The money talk — pin down 3 things in writing

Standard India recruitment fee: a percentage of the candidate's annual salary, commonly around 8.33% (one month's salary), ranging roughly 6–12%. Bulk BPO is often a flat fee per joiner. Use the Fee Calculator tab to quote.

Before sending a single profile, get these confirmed (an email reply is enough):

  • The fee — the % or flat amount.
  • Replacement guarantee — if the candidate quits within ~90 days, you replace free.
  • When you get paid — normally on joining date, invoice with a 30-day window.
"Confirming our terms: 8.33% on joining, 90-day replacement, payment within 30 days." — get them to reply "confirmed." That reply is your protection.
5. The negative poles — how agencies get cheated (and the defense)
Backdoor hiring (the big one). You send a profile, the company hires them directly and claims no fee.
Defense: never send a profile without agreed terms first. Header every profile "Submitted by SVS Consultancy on [date], subject to our terms." Keep the dated email. Log it in the Submission Log.
"We already have this candidate." Sometimes true, often an excuse.
Defense: your dated submission email settles it — first to submit with proof wins.
Ghost / fake requirements. No real intent — collecting profiles to map salaries or pressure their vendor.
Spot it: vague JD, no urgency, won't share interview process or timeline, keeps asking for "more profiles" but never schedules.
Defense: ask how many positions, the interview process, the timeline, who makes the final call. Real openings have real answers.
Non-payment after joining. Candidate joins, invoice gets dodged.
Defense: only extend trust to companies that confirmed terms. Follow up firmly on the agreed date.
"Prove yourself first" trap. "Place one free, then we'll sign." They ghost after.
Defense: terms before profiles, always. A serious client confirms terms over email without fuss.
Fake HR. The "HR" is fishing for candidate data or trying to charge your candidates.
Defense: verify — real company email domain (not random Gmail), real LinkedIn, real office. If an "HR" ever asks your candidate to pay, it's a scam. Walk.
The thread through all six: terms in writing before profiles, and a dated record of every submission. Do these two religiously and you've closed off most ways to get cheated.
6. Protect your own reputation
  • Never promise a candidate a job in exchange for money. Sell training as training.
  • Keep the SVS name and contact consistent everywhere — mismatched names read as fly-by-night.
  • Be anonymous as a person if you like, but the business must be named and reachable. No company pays an invoice to an anonymous account.
